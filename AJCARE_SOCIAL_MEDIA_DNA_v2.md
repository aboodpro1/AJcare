This identity is stable. It does not change based on trends, formats, or quarterly performance metrics. It is expressed differently across contexts, but it remains recognizable and consistent.

## 03 — CONTENT STRATEGY

### Core Social Purpose: Education → Trust → Product Guidance → Purchase

This is the strategic foundation for all social content.

**Stage 1 — Education**  
Content solves real audience problems and provides useful understanding. Examples: how-to, mechanism explanation, ingredient education, skincare routines, product selection guidance. The audience learns something valuable.

**Stage 2 — Trust**  
Consistent, honest, scientifically responsible education builds trust. Transparency about sources, acknowledgment of uncertainty, refusal to fabricate claims, and genuine desire to help create credibility. The audience begins to believe AJCare understands skincare and can be relied upon.

**Stage 3 — Product Guidance**  
Once trust exists, product recommendations become credible. Instead of "Buy this product," the message is "This product solves the problem we just discussed." Product communication flows naturally from the education that preceded it.

**Stage 4 — Purchase**  
A trusted source recommending a relevant solution creates purchase intent. However, this stage is a consequence of the first three stages, not the primary driver.

**Core Principle:** Never reverse this sequence. Never start with promotion hoping to manufacture trust. Education must come first.

### Do Not Transform Social Into a Discount Store

AJCare social media is not:
- A constant sales channel
- A promotional catalog
- A trend-chasing content factory
- A daily discount announcement stream
- A pharmacy bulk-order alert system

AJCare social media is:
- A premium skincare and pharmaceutical education environment
- A brand authority voice in scientific skincare communication
- A source of useful information that happens to exist for a business
- A builder of genuine audience relationships

### Organic vs. Advertising Communication

**Organic Social Communication (Post Feed)**
- Primary purpose: Education and trust-building
- Audience-centered approach
- Useful information first, commercial intent secondary
- Authority-building through consistent value delivery
- Direct selling does not dominate

**Advertising (Paid Posts)**
- May be more commercially explicit
- Can promote specific products more directly
- Can communicate B2B/wholesale opportunities
- Can use discount/urgency signals (though this is not preferred)
- Still maintain consumer value as primary creative layer

**Rule:** Do not allow B2B messaging to turn AJCare's consumer-facing social identity into a separate brand or a sales-only channel. The organic social audience and the B2B commercial direction are related but distinct. Organic social serves the consumer audience primarily.

### Content Philosophy: Problem-Solving

AJCare content should primarily solve a real problem or address a real need that your audience actually has.

**Preferred Strategic Structure (Not Mandatory Template)**
```
Problem / Pain Point
    ↓
Why This Happens / Mechanism Understanding
    ↓
Solution / Action / Next Steps
```

This structure works for complex topics. However, some topics may appropriately use simpler structures. Do not force complexity into simple ideas.

**Example Applications:**
- *Topic: Acne-prone skin* → People with acne want to understand what causes it (mechanism) → They need solutions that actually work (product or routine)
- *Topic: Sensitive skin* → People with sensitive skin want to know what causes reactions (mechanism) → They need safe product recommendations and barrier-repair strategies
- *Topic: Korean skincare layering* → People are confused about order (problem) → Understanding layering logic helps them build effective routines (mechanism/solution)

### Content Categories and Treatment

AJCare creates content in two main categories. Each requires appropriate scientific rigor.

**Category 01: Cosmetics / Skincare**
- Ingredients and formulations
- Skin concerns and skin health
- Skincare routines and product selection
- Korean skincare education
- Product guidance and recommendations
- How-to and application

Treatment: Evidence-based but typically less regulated than pharmaceuticals. Can use ingredients research, consumer research, expert guidance, and verified user experience.

**Category 02: Medications / Pharmaceuticals**
- Medication education and mechanism of action
- Dosage, usage, and timing
- Risks and adverse effects
- Toxicity and organ effects
- Regulatory and prescription information

Treatment: High scientific rigor. Requires verified medical evidence. Cannot present opinions as facts. Must acknowledge regulatory boundaries (when a medication requires a prescription, content must respect that). Uncertainty must be explicitly preserved.

**Unified Brand, Differentiated Treatment**  
The brand identity remains unified across both categories. However, the scientific rigor and communication approach must match the safety-sensitivity of each category.

**Never Blur Categories**  
Do not present cosmetic ingredients as medications. Do not present medications as casual skincare. Do not invent medical claims for cosmetic products.

## 04 — CONTENT MATRIX

### What Is the Content Matrix?

The Content Matrix is the operational translation of Content Strategy into actual content production. It defines:
- What topics AJCare communicates about
- What angles/approaches exist for each topic
- What formats are appropriate for each angle
- How individual content ideas are understood and developed
- How ideas are validated before production

### Content Pillars (Core Topic Areas)

Based on AJCare's Brand DNA and Strategic Direction, content primarily covers:

**Pillar 01: Skincare Education**
Ingredients, mechanisms, skin types, common concerns (acne, sensitivity, aging), routine structure, product selection logic, Korean skincare approach. Primary audience value: understanding how to choose and use skincare effectively.

**Pillar 02: Product Information & Guidance**
How to use specific AJCare products, what problems they solve, how they fit into routines, ingredient explanations, when/why to choose one product over another. Primary value: Making informed product decisions.

**Pillar 03: Pharmaceutical/Medical Information**
Medication education, mechanism of action, safety considerations, proper usage. Typically focused on products AJCare distributes. Primary value: Understanding how medications work and using them correctly.

**Pillar 04: Credibility & Expertise**
Research sharing, scientific accuracy, expert collaboration, evidence transparency, thought leadership. Primary value: Establishing AJCare as a trustworthy source.

**Pillar 05: Audience Engagement & Community**
Questions, polls, audience input, shared experiences, practical challenges. Not primary pillar. Primary value: Building relationships and understanding audience problems.

**Rule:** Do not invent additional pillars merely to make the system appear comprehensive. These five pillars align with AJCare's brand identity and business direction. If a new pillar is strategically required, it requires explicit decision-making and Brand DNA alignment check.

### Content Angles (Approaches Within Each Pillar)

Each pillar can be approached from multiple angles. Angles are the *perspective* or *hook* used to address a topic.

**Example: Skincare Education Pillar**

| Angle | Approach | Example Topic |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Explain how something works | "How does retinol actually work on skin?" |
| **Comparison** | Show differences and choices | "Retinol vs. Retinoid: What's the difference?" |
| **Routine** | Address sequence and structure | "The 10-step Korean skincare routine explained" |
| **Problem-Solution** | Address specific skin concerns | "Best ingredients for acne-prone skin" |
| **Science-To-Practice** | Move from research to real application | "Lab findings about hyaluronic acid + how to actually use it" |
| **Myth-Busting** | Correct misconceptions | "Myths about oily skin that are actually wrong" |
| **Ingredient Deep-Dive** | Focus on specific ingredient | "Everything about niacinamide" |

Angles create variety while staying within strategic focus. They help avoid repetitive messaging while addressing real audience needs.

### Format Selection Logic

**Critical Sequence (Never Reverse)**
```
UNDERSTAND THE IDEA
    ↓
DEVELOP THE CONCEPT
    ↓
VALIDATE THE CONCEPT
    ↓
SELECT THE BEST FORMAT
```

**Never:**
1. Choose a format first and then force an idea into it
2. Select format based on what you think will perform best
3. Default to the same format repeatedly
4. Choose format based on trend or what other brands are doing

**Always:**
1. Understand what you're trying to communicate
2. Develop the core idea fully
3. Validate that the idea is worth producing
4. Only then ask: What is the best way to communicate this?

### Directional Format Guidelines

These are guidelines, not automatic rules. The best format depends on the specific idea.

| If the content is... | Preferred format is... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Visual sequencing / step-by-step / layering | Carousel | Multiple slides allow step progression |
| Movement / demonstration / showing how to apply | Reel / Video | Motion communicates application better than static |
| Quick education / single fact / context-dependent | Story | Time-limited, conversational, quick consumption |
| Simple information / single idea / text-focused | Static Post | No format overhead needed |
| Quick engagement / question / poll | Story > Comments | Faster interaction |
| Complex mechanism / deep education | Carousel > Long-form Reel | Allows proper explanation depth |
| Emotional / aspirational / brand mood | Reel / Visual-heavy Static | Motion and design communicate brand feel |
| Product showcase / detailed features | Carousel > Static | Multiple angles and information |

### Content Idea Interpretation System

Every content idea must pass through an interpretation system before development begins. This prevents misunderstanding and wasted effort.

**Input:** Raw idea or user request  
**Output:** Understood concept ready for development

**Interpretation Steps:**

**Step 01: Capture Input**
Accept the raw idea. It may be well-formed or vague. Do not assume it is complete.

*Examples of input quality:*
- "Let's do a carousel about retinol" (vague)
- "I've gotten a lot of questions about whether double cleansing is necessary" (clear problem, clear audience need)
- "A reel about skincare for sensitive skin" (vague direction)

**Step 02: Understand Intention**
Ask clarifying questions to understand:
- What problem is being addressed?
- Who is the target audience for this specific piece?
- What does the audience need to understand or do?
- Why now? Is there a current business reason or audience need driving this?

*Example clarification for vague idea:*
- User input: "Let's do a carousel about retinol"
- Questions: "What aspect of retinol? Are we explaining retinoid types, how to use it, its benefits, or addressing common confusion?" "What audience — beginners or experienced users?" "Is there a business reason or does this address a question we've received?"

**Step 03: Extract Core Idea**
Distill the intention into a single, clear idea statement.

*Example extraction:*
- Input: "Let's do retinol carousel"
- Core idea: "Explain why retinol is effective for aging skin and how to use it without irritation — target audience is people new to retinol who are intimidated by it"

**Step 04: Analyze the Problem / Need**
What real audience problem does this address?
- Is it a commonly asked question?
- Is it a real barrier to good skincare decisions?
- Is it confusion that prevents proper product use?
- Is it a gap in audience knowledge?

*Example problem analysis:*
- Problem: "People avoid retinol because they fear irritation or don't understand how to start"
- Audience need: "Clear explanation of retinol benefits + practical, step-by-step guidance on introduction"
- Strategic fit: "Supports education pillar and product guidance"

**Step 05: Develop / Enrich**
Add strategic thinking to the core idea:
- What angle will we use? (Mechanism? Problem-solution? Myth-busting?)
- What structure makes sense? (Problem → Why → Solution?)
- What specific information needs to be included?
- Are there misconceptions to address?
- What's the appropriate evidence level?

*Example enrichment:*
- Core idea: "Explain retinol + how to use it"
- Enriched concept: "Address retinol fear by explaining mechanism (how it works on aging), acknowledge real irritation risk (honest), provide step-by-step introduction protocol (practical), include AJCare product option that fits (but only if it actually does)"

**Step 06: Formulate the Concept**
Create a brief concept statement that includes:
- Core message
- Target audience
- Intended angle
- Validation status (ready to validate or needs further development)

*Example formulation:*
- **Concept:** "Educational carousel on retinol introduction for beginners anxious about irritation. Angle: Science-based explanation + practical protocol. Audience: Women 25-40 concerned about aging who have avoided retinol. Message: Retinol is effective but must be introduced properly. Format: Carousel (6 slides, problem → mechanism → protocol → product option → expected timeline). Status: Ready for validation."

**Step 07: Clarification Rule**

If the intention is unclear after asking targeted clarifying questions:
- State this explicitly
- Do not invent user intent
- Ask the user to restate or refine the idea

*Example:*
- "I understand you want retinol content, but I'm unclear whether this is addressing retinoid types, application technique, or overcoming reluctance to use retinol. Can you clarify which problem your audience is actually facing?"

### Validation System

After an idea is understood and a concept is developed, it enters the Validation System before format selection or production.

**Validation Sequence (Strict Order)**

The validator evaluates the developed concept in this specific priority order:

**01 — Audience Need Validation**  
*Question:* Does this address a real and meaningful problem for our audience?

- Is this a question we've received multiple times?
- Is this confusion that prevents good skincare decisions?
- Is this a barrier to product use or understanding?
- Is this something the audience actually cares about?

**Outcome:**
- PASS: This addresses a real need
- FAIL: This is not a real problem the audience has. REJECT the concept.

**02 — Value Validation**  
*Question:* Will the audience gain useful understanding from this content?

- Will they learn something actionable?
- Will they be able to make better decisions after this content?
- Is the information valuable enough to justify attention?
- Is this teaching them something or just filling time?

**Outcome:**
- PASS: This delivers real value
- FAIL: This is low-value or obvious information. REVISE to add more depth or REJECT.

**03 — Evidence Validation**  
*Question:* Is the information scientifically defensible?

- Can we support the claims with research, expert consensus, or verifiable information?
- Is this opinion, or is it evidence-based?
- For pharmaceutical content, can we cite proper medical sources?
- For cosmetic content, can we cite ingredient research or verified product information?
- If evidence is mixed, can we communicate the uncertainty?

**Outcome:**
- PASS: Evidence exists and supports the concept
- FAIL: We cannot defend this information. REJECT the concept.
- REVISION: Evidence exists but needs proper uncertainty/caveat language. REVISE the concept.

**04 — Strategic Fit Validation**  
*Question:* Does this fit AJCare's current direction and brand position?

- Does this support education-first positioning?
- Does this align with brand personality (Luxury · Scientific · Honest · Modern · Confident · Caring)?
- Does this fit AJCare's business stage and credibility level?
- For product content: Does the product actually solve the problem discussed?

**Outcome:**
- PASS: Fits strategic direction
- FAIL: This contradicts brand positioning or business stage. REJECT or REVISE.

**05 — Attention Potential Validation**  
*Question:* Does this concept have credible attention potential?

- Will the hook engage the target audience?
- Is there genuine curiosity/interest to draw people in?
- Is this something people will want to share or save?
- Note: "Attention potential" is not the primary decision driver, but it matters for content that's meant to reach people

**Outcome:**
- PASS: The concept has engagement potential
- CONCERN: This is valuable but may not reach many people. Proceed if value is high enough OR improve the hook/angle.
- FAIL: Even with good value, this seems completely uninteresting. Consider if a different angle would work better.

**Validator Output**

After all five validations, the validator outputs one of three decisions:

| Decision | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **APPROVE** | Passes all validations. Concept is ready for format selection and production. | Proceed to Step 05 (Format Selection) and production. |
| **REVISE** | Valuable concept but needs specific changes before production. Validator clearly states what needs to change. | Make revisions and re-validate OR confirm with user that revisions are acceptable. |
| **REJECT** | Does not meet validation criteria. Not worth producing in current form. | Concept is removed from queue. If user believes it should be reconsidered, they can request re-development with new direction. |

**Validation as Real Decision Layer**

The validation system is not decorative. It must function as an actual decision-making layer.

- Do not rubber-stamp validation
- Do not rush through validator steps
- Rejection is a legitimate and necessary outcome
- Revisions should be specific and achievable
- The validation output should surprise no one — criteria are clear, objective, and aligned with AJCare's strategy

## 05 — DESIGN SYSTEM

### Visual Communication DNA

AJCare's visual expression combines:

**Luxury + Scientific + Modern + Minimal + Editorial**

This means:

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Color** | Curated, refined palette (established in Brand DNA). No garish or high-saturation colors. Premium aesthetic. |
| **Typography** | Sophisticated typefaces. Proper hierarchy. Mobile readability protected. Consistent sizing scales. |
| **Layout** | Clean, organized, high use of negative space. No cramped or dense layouts. Visual breathing room. |
| **Photography/Imagery** | High-quality, editorial, premium. Not stock-photo generic. Consistent style. Authenticity where appropriate. |
| **Visual Hierarchy** | Clear distinction between headline, supporting information, and detail. Audience knows where to look first. |
| **Spacing & Margins** | Generous spacing. Intentional alignment. Organized grid. Never cramped. |
| **Decoration** | Minimal and purposeful. Decorative elements must serve the design, not just fill space. |
| **Movement (Reels/Video)** | Smooth, intentional, premium. No jittery or low-quality motion. Serves communication, not distraction. |

### Principle: Visual = Part of the Explanation

Visuals are not decoration. Every visual element must serve a communication purpose.

**Good Visual Hierarchy:**
- Image shows the concept being explained
- Text explains the mechanism
- Together, they teach more than either alone

**Bad Visual Hierarchy:**
- Image fills space
- Text explains everything
- Image could be replaced with any other image and nothing changes

**Examples:**

*Good:* Carousel about retinoid types → Each slide uses an illustration showing the chemical structure/effect difference between types → Visuals communicate mechanism that text alone might not make clear.

*Good:* Carousel about K-beauty layering → Step-by-step images show actual application order → Visuals demonstrate the sequence that text lists.

*Bad:* Carousel about acne causes → Every slide has a large image of skin → Images are decorative; text could stand alone.

*Bad:* Product post → Large product photo → Small text about the product → Photo is just product showcase, not communication.

### Information-First Design Philosophy

**Core Principle:** Education first. Visuals support education.

Never sacrifice:
- Information clarity
- Readability (especially mobile)
- Scientific accuracy
- Typography quality
- Hierarchy
- Comprehension

for:
- Visual spectacle
- Decorative complexity
- Trend-driven styling
- Excessive imagery
- Unnecessary animation
- Aesthetic novelty

**Test:** Could your grandmother read and understand this post on a phone? If not, the design has failed.

### Language Direction System (MANDATORY NEW RULE)

This is a critical operational rule that governs all visual design.

#### Arabic Content: Complete RTL Visual System

When the primary content language is **Arabic**, the entire visual composition must be evaluated and adapted as **Right-to-Left (RTL)**.

RTL is not just text direction. It fundamentally affects visual composition.

**RTL applies to:**

- **Text direction** — Reads right to left
- **Text alignment** — Aligns to the right
- **Layout direction** — Visual flow moves right to left
- **Composition flow** — Information hierarchy reads right-to-left
- **Visual hierarchy** — Primary information on the right, secondary on left
- **Information flow** — Eye path follows RTL reading pattern
- **Element positioning** — Buttons, CTAs positioned for RTL eye flow
- **Icon positioning** — Directional icons (arrows, pointers) face left
- **Arrows & directional elements** — Point left (direction of reading flow)
- **Visual sequences** — If showing steps, sequence flows right-to-left
- **Decorative elements** — Positioned for RTL balance
- **Image/text relationships** — Images positioned to support RTL reading flow
- **Internal slide structure** — For carousels, each slide's internal layout is RTL
- **Reading path** — First element is on the right, last on the left
- **CTA positioning** — Call-to-action positioned where RTL eye naturally ends
- **Supporting elements** — All supporting text, icons, decorations adapt to RTL

**CRITICAL RULE:**

Do **NOT** take an existing English/LTR design and simply replace the English text with Arabic.

This creates:
```
LTR TEMPLATE + ARABIC TEXT = Visual mismatch
```

This is wrong. The audience immediately feels that Arabic was "added on" rather than designed for.

**Correct approach:**

Think about the content in Arabic first. Then design the visual system for Arabic. The result should be a genuine RTL composition.

```
ARABIC CONTENT + RTL VISUAL COMPOSITION = Native, intentional design
```

**Example — Carousel Slide for Retinol Education (Arabic version)**

❌ **WRONG:**
- Left-to-right layout
- Arabic text inserted
- Icons and arrows pointing right
- CTA on the left
- English design, Arabic text

✅ **CORRECT:**
- Right-to-left layout
- Arabic text naturally positioned
- Icons and arrows pointing left
- CTA on the right
- Full RTL visual system

#### English Content: Complete LTR Visual System

When the primary content language is **English**, the entire visual composition must be evaluated and adapted as **Left-to-Right (LTR)**.

**LTR applies to:**

- **Text direction** — Reads left to right
- **Text alignment** — Aligns to the left
- **Layout direction** — Visual flow moves left to right
- **Information hierarchy** — Primary information on left, secondary on right
- **Visual hierarchy** — Eye path follows LTR reading pattern
- **Element positioning** — Buttons, CTAs positioned for LTR eye flow
- **Icon positioning** — Directional icons point right
- **Arrows & directional elements** — Point right (direction of reading flow)
- **Visual sequences** — If showing steps, sequence flows left-to-right
- **Reading path** — First element is on the left, last on the right
- **CTA positioning** — Call-to-action positioned where LTR eye naturally ends
- **Image/text relationships** — Images positioned to support LTR reading flow
- **Internal layout** — All elements organized for left-to-right scanning

**CRITICAL RULE:**

Do **NOT** create an LTR design and simply insert English text into it, expecting it to work as a multilingual asset.

Create genuine LTR compositions for English content.

#### Bilingual Design Principle

Language adaptation is not translation. It's design thinking that starts with language direction.

**Wrong mental model:**
```
Language → Replace Text → Same design for all languages
```

**Correct mental model:**
```
Language → Reading Direction → Visual Direction → Composition → Hierarchy → Element Positioning
```

**Never:**
- Use the same design layout for Arabic and English
- Insert text in different languages into the same visual template
- Assume that "just flipping the design" creates proper RTL

**Always:**
- Design separately for each language's reading direction
- Evaluate how each language's flow affects visual hierarchy
- Test readability and flow in each language independently
- Ensure Arabic feels native (not inserted), and English feels native (not inserted)

**This rule applies to:**
- Static posts
- Carousel slides
- Reel covers and text overlays
- Story designs
- Product posts
- Educational posts
- All future specialized formats

### Carousel Architecture

A carousel is a short, intentional journey. Each slide must have a reason to exist.

**Carousel Purpose:**
Enable multi-slide storytelling that teaches sequentially, builds understanding progressively, and maintains engagement across multiple slides.

**Carousel Maximum Length:**
7 slides maximum. This is a hard ceiling.

Never add filler slides to reach a certain number. If you can communicate the idea in 4 slides, use 4 slides. Do not artificially extend to 7.

**Carousel Slide Rules:**

Each slide must:
- Serve a specific communication purpose
- Build on previous understanding
- Be readable on mobile (the primary viewing device)
- Have clear visual hierarchy
- Be self-contained (readable as a single image)
- But also fit within the sequence

**Preferred Carousel Narrative (Not Mandatory)**

The most effective carousel structure follows this flow:

1. **Hook** — Capture attention and set context
   - Why should they care?
   - What problem/question does this address?
   - What will they learn?

2. **Problem / Pain Point** — Establish the audience need
   - What confusion exists?
   - What's the barrier?
   - What do people misunderstand?

3. **Curiosity / "Why?"** — Invite deeper thinking
   - Why does this problem exist?
   - What's the mechanism?
   - Why should understanding this matter?

4. **Understanding / Mechanism** — Explain the science or logic
   - Here's how/why
   - Here's the research/evidence
   - Here's the principle

5. **Value / Application** — Connect to real use
   - What does this mean practically?
   - How does this help?
   - What should they do differently?

6. **Resolution / Action** — Close with clear next step
   - Try this
   - Choose this
   - Remember this
   - (Optional: relevant AJCare product)

7. (Optional) **CTA / Engagement** — Final call-to-action
   - Only if the journey needs it
   - Can be implicit in Slide 6

**Examples of valid alternative structures:**

- *Problem → Myths → Truth → Application* (myth-busting focus)
- *Comparison A → Comparison B → Comparison C → When to use each* (comparison focus)
- *Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 → Step 4 → Result* (how-to focus)
- *What is it? → How does it work? → How to use? → Results* (education focus)

**Carousel Length Examples:**

- Simple topic (3-4 slides): "What is niacinamide?" → Mechanism → Benefits → How to use
- Medium topic (5-6 slides): "Retinol for beginners" → Why retinol? → Types → How to start → What to expect → Product option
- Complex topic (6-7 slides): "Korean skincare steps explained" → Why layering? → Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 → How to personalize → Getting started

Never force complexity into unnecessary slides.

### Visual Hierarchy & Information Density

**Principle:** Visual hierarchy must be clear. The audience should know what to read first, second, and third.

**Hierarchy Elements:**

| Priority | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Headline / Primary message** | Largest text, most prominent position, strongest contrast, clearest typeface |
| **Supporting information** | Medium text size, secondary position, moderate contrast |
| **Detail / Fine print** | Smallest text, least prominent position, lowest contrast (if included) |

**Mobile Reading Path:**

On mobile (primary viewing device), the reading path must be intuitive:
1. What's the headline? (clear, large, top or center)
2. What's the supporting info? (medium, secondary position)
3. Is there more detail? (small, tertiary position)

**Information Density:**

Information-heavy content (carousels, educational posts) should maintain approximately:

**~65% Information / Text** | **~35% Supporting Visuals**

This is a strategic guideline, not a mathematical pixel requirement.

**Intent of the ratio:**
- Information remains dominant
- Visuals support understanding
- Visuals clarify rather than distract
- Design does not become image-heavy at expense of education
- Audience gets substance, not just pretty pictures

**Do NOT artificially fill the 35% visual portion.**

If a visual does not improve understanding, it should not exist merely for decoration.

**Examples:**

✅ **Good information/visual balance** (Educational carousel)
- Slide has headline (large, clear)
- Slide has 2-3 key information points (medium text, organized)
- Slide has 1-2 supporting visuals or icons (clarify the information)
- Result: Information is dominant; visuals support

❌ **Bad information/visual balance** (Over-designed carousel)
- Slide is 70% large imagery
- Text is squeezed into small spaces
- Audience must strain to read information
- Visuals are decorative, not educational

### Typography

Typography is a communication system, not just a styling choice.

**Typography purposes:**
- Create hierarchy (large = important, small = detail)
- Support readability (especially on mobile)
- Reflect brand personality (luxury requires appropriate typefaces)
- Enable language direction (Arabic and English typefaces must be chosen for their respective languages)
- Distinguish types of content (headline vs. body vs. supporting)

**Typography rules:**

**01 — Hierarchy Through Size**
- Primary message: Largest (typically 24-32px on mobile)
- Supporting information: Medium (typically 14-18px on mobile)
- Detail: Small (typically 12px or smaller on mobile)

**02 — Readability First**
- Minimum 12px body text on mobile
- High contrast between text and background
- Line spacing sufficient for comfort (not cramped)
- Line length not excessively long on desktop (readability suffers after ~60 characters)

**03 — Typeface Selection for Arabic**
- Choose typefaces designed for Arabic readability
- Avoid pairing English serif fonts with Arabic; choose complementary Arabic typefaces
- Test Arabic readability on mobile (smaller devices require more generous spacing)
- Ensure Arabic typeface has sufficient weight to remain legible at display sizes

**04 — Typeface Selection for English**
- Choose typefaces appropriate for brand luxury positioning
- Clarity over creativity
- Web-safe fonts or Google Fonts to ensure consistency across devices
- Pair serif and sans-serif intentionally

**05 — Scientific Terminology**
- Use correct terminology (e.g., "retinoid," not "retinol," when referring to prescription vitamin A derivatives)
- For mixed Arabic/English text, keep terminology consistent and properly formatted
- Ensure scientific terms are readable in the chosen typeface

**Do NOT invent specific font names unless they are already established in AJCare Brand DNA.**

If exact fonts are not specified in Brand DNA, define the behavioral requirement instead of fabricating a font choice.

*Example:*
- ❌ Wrong: "Use Montserrat for headers"  (invented if not in Brand DNA)
- ✅ Right: "Use a clean, modern sans-serif typeface for headers, chosen from established brand options or web-safe alternatives"

### Layout System

Layout governs how content is organized visually.

**Layout principles:**

**01 — Grid & Alignment**
- Content aligns to an intentional grid
- Consistency in margins and spacing
- Organized, structured appearance
- No random element placement

**02 — Spacing & Margins**
- Generous negative space
- Breathing room between elements
- Not cramped or dense
- Whitespace is intentional, not accidental

**03 — Text/Image Relationship**
- Image positions to support reading flow
- Text positioned for clarity
- Together, they communicate (not separately)
- Mobile layout differs from desktop (intentionally)

**04 — Directional Behavior**
- For Arabic (RTL): Layout flows right-to-left
- For English (LTR): Layout flows left-to-right
- Entire composition adapted, not just text direction

**05 — Responsive Design**
- Content readable on mobile (primary device)
- Adapts to different screen sizes
- Buttons/CTAs are tap-able on mobile (minimum 44px)
- Text remains readable at all sizes

**06 — Density Variation**
- Complex information: Lower density, more space
- Simple information: Can be denser (but never cramped)
- Educational content: Moderate density (not overwhelming)
- Promotional content: Can be denser (but information remains clear)

**Principle: Uniform DNA → Variable Execution**

All AJCare content should be recognizably AJCare through consistent visual DNA (color, typography, spacing principles, hierarchy rules). However, templates can vary in specific execution as long as the underlying design logic remains recognizable.

## 06 — CONTENT GENERATION

### What Content Generation Means

Content Generation is the execution layer. It converts a validated concept (from Content Matrix) into an actual post/carousel/reel/story ready for publishing.

Content Generation must:
1. Preserve Brand Foundation principles
2. Follow Content Strategy (education-first, problem-solving)
3. Use structures defined in Content Matrix
4. Execute design according to Design System
5. Pass Quality Gates before publishing
6. Protect credibility and never fabricate facts

### Operational Generation Rules

**Rule 01 — Never Invent Information**

Never invent:
- Medical information or medical claims
- Scientific research or citations
- Customer testimonials or reviews
- Pharmacy partnerships or relationships
- Sales numbers or business traction
- Expert endorsements or credentials
- Certifications or qualifications
- Market data or assumptions

If information is unavailable, mark it as:
- "Decision Required" (business decision not yet made)
- "Required Input" (factual information needed)
- "Validation Required" (assumption needs verification)
- "To be added" (will be filled in when available)

**Do not silently treat assumptions as facts.**

**Rule 02 — Preserve Uncertainty**

When scientific evidence is mixed or incomplete:
- Acknowledge the uncertainty explicitly
- Do not present one viewpoint as settled fact
- Communicate complexity honestly
- Preserve the scientific principle: "If we do not know, say we do not know"

**Example:**
- ❌ WRONG: "Hyaluronic acid is proven to reverse aging"
- ✅ RIGHT: "Research suggests hyaluronic acid helps skin retain moisture, which can improve hydration-related appearance. However, 'reversal' of aging is complex and depends on many factors."

**Rule 03 — Research Requirements**

Research depth must adapt to topic complexity and risk level.

| Topic Type | Research Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Simple cosmetic fact** (e.g., "What is niacinamide?") | Moderate: Brand ingredient info + 1-2 research sources |
| **Cosmetic mechanism** (e.g., "How does retinol work?") | Higher: Multiple sources, verify mechanism accuracy |
| **Pharmaceutical info** (e.g., "How to use medication") | Very High: Medical sources, verify regulatory status, acknowledge prescription requirements |
| **Health claims** (e.g., "X ingredient reduces inflammation") | Very High: Clinical evidence required, not anecdotal |
| **Controversial topics** (e.g., "Controversial skincare ingredient") | Very High: Multiple viewpoints, acknowledge debate, do not present opinion as fact |

**Rule 04 — Brand Consistency in Content**

Every piece of content must reflect:
- Brand personality (Luxury · Scientific · Honest · Modern · Confident · Caring)
- Voice consistency (familiar but authoritative)
- Visual consistency (recognizable as AJCare)
- Educational focus (solves a real problem)
- Credibility (never fabricates facts)

**Rule 05 — Language-Specific Adaptation**

Arabic and English content must be adapted, not translated.

For the same idea expressed in both languages:
- Core message stays consistent
- Examples may differ (culturally appropriate)
- Phrasing is natural for each language
- Hooks may vary to match audience interest
- CTAs may be different (but strategic intent aligned)

**Example:**
- **Arabic version hook:** "ليش طريقة التنظيف الكوري تختلف عن الطرق الأخرى" (Why is Korean cleansing different?)
- **English version hook:** "Why Korean skincare uses double cleansing (and you might too)"

Same concept, different hooks.

### Quality Gates (Before Publishing)

Every piece of content must pass seven quality gates. Content that fails any gate does not publish.

| Gate | Question | Pass Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **01 Idea Gate** | Is the idea understood and is user intention preserved? | Idea has been interpreted, clarified, and approved. Intention is clear. |
| **02 Audience Gate** | Does this address a real and meaningful audience need? | Audience problem/question is real and meaningful. This content solves it. |
| **03 Evidence Gate** | Is the information scientifically/factually defensible? | Claims are backed by evidence. Uncertainty is acknowledged. No fabrication. |
| **04 Strategy Gate** | Does this fit AJCare's current direction and business stage? | Aligns with brand identity, education-first positioning, and current credibility level. |
| **05 Communication Gate** | Is the format appropriate? Can the idea be clearly communicated? | Format chosen correctly. Idea is clear and understandable. No confusion. |
| **06 Brand Gate** | Does it preserve AJCare's personality and credibility? | Content reflects Luxury · Scientific · Honest · Modern · Confident · Caring. Builds trust, not hype. |
| **07 Visual Gate** | Does the visual design communicate meaning? Is it mobile-readable? | Visuals support understanding. Text is readable on mobile. Language direction is correct (RTL for Arabic, LTR for English). |

**Gate Logic:**
- ALL gates must pass
- Failure on any gate = content does not publish
- Revision is the result of gate failure (make specific changes and re-gate)
- Rejection is when gate failure cannot be resolved

### Credibility Governance (Core Principle)

AJCare builds real credibility, never manufactured credibility.

**Never create:**
- Fake customer testimonials
- Fake reviews or ratings
- Fake pharmacy partnerships
- Fake orders or sales conversations
- Fake WhatsApp screenshots or "real customer chats"
- Fake distributor relationships
- Fake certifications or lab results
- Fake expert endorsements
- Invented research claims

**Operating principle from AJCare Operating Plan:**
*"AJ Care must build real credibility, not manufactured credibility. Never create fake customers, testimonials, pharmacy partnerships, orders, conversations, sales numbers, distributor claims, or certifications. Real social proof comes only from real market activity."*

**This is non-negotiable.**

Early-stage businesses have limited social proof. That's acceptable. Fabricating it is not.

**Appropriate credibility claims for early-stage:**
- ✅ "AJCare carries premium Korean skincare brands: [names]"
- ✅ "Backed by ingredients research: [specific studies linked]"
- ✅ "Serving customers in [verified locations]" (if true)
- ✅ "Product recommended by [real expert, with permission]" (if explicitly given permission)
- ❌ "Trusted by thousands of customers" (if not verified)
- ❌ "5,000+ satisfied customers" (if made up)
- ❌ "Pharmacy partnership with [pharmacy name]" (if not formally established)

### Performance & Evolution

**Performance is a learning signal, not an automatic rule generator.**

**What Performance Data Can Tell Us:**
- Which topics resonate with the audience
- Which formats engage best
- Which hooks work
- Which visuals communicate more effectively
- What questions the audience is asking
- Emerging interest areas

**What Performance Data Cannot Tell Us:**
- That the Social Media DNA should change
- That a single well-performing post justifies strategy shift
- That a trend is a business direction
- That a format should become the default

**Evolution Principle: Accumulated Evidence**

The Social Media DNA changes only when:

1. **Accumulated Evidence exists** (not one-off performance)
   - Multiple pieces of data pointing in same direction
   - Consistent pattern over time
   - Meaningful sample size

2. **Meaningful Learning has occurred**
   - Understanding of *why* the pattern exists
   - Implications for brand strategy
   - Alignment with business goals

3. **Clear Strategic Reason exists**
   - Change improves alignment with brand DNA
   - Change improves audience value
   - Change improves business outcomes
   - Change is documented and justified

**Evolution Flow:**
```
PERFORMANCE DATA
    ↓
ANALYSIS & LEARNING
    ↓
ACCUMULATED EVIDENCE
    ↓
STRATEGIC INTERPRETATION
    ↓
IS DNA CHANGE JUSTIFIED?
    ↓
If YES: Explicit decision + documentation + implementation
If NO: Learning recorded, DNA stays stable
```

**Examples of unjustified DNA changes:**
- ❌ One carousel performed well → "Make all content carousels"
- ❌ A trending topic got engagement → "Change brand positioning"
- ❌ One Reel went viral → "Replace educational content with trend content"
- ❌ Discount-heavy post sold something → "Become a discount brand"

**Examples of justified DNA changes:**
- ✅ Consistent pattern: Video-first carousels outperform text-first carousels across 20+ pieces over 3 months → Refine carousel slide design to emphasize visuals
- ✅ Accumulated evidence: Audiences ask repeated questions about K-beauty + this content pillar consistently underperforms → Expand K-beauty education pillar
- ✅ Strategic shift: Business moves into pharmacy B2B as primary channel (business decision) → Create specialized content channel for pharmacy audience (separate social or specialized content stream)
